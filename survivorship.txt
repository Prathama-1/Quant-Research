SURVIVORSHIP BIAS IN THE TASK 2 UNIVERSE
Prathama Bharadwaj


THE PROBLEM

The 555 stocks are those liquid in October 2026, so membership looks into the future.
Every stock in the file is still trading on the last date, 28 Jun 2024. Stocks that
failed, merged or dried up after 2021 are missing, and so are IPOs that flopped. Stocks
that were small in 2022 are included only because they later grew.


WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE IN THE DATA

1. The whole universe beats the index. A daily-rebalanced equal-weight portfolio of all
   listed names made +148% before costs in the window (Sharpe 2.48), against +44.6% for
   Nifty. Only 11.4% of names fell.

2. Random stocks nearly reach the bar. 15 random liquid names, monthly, average Sharpe
   1.66 (std 0.36, 24 seeds). The firm's reference of 1.93 is less than one standard
   deviation above chance on this universe, so the Sharpe > 1.9 bar says little about
   skill.

3. The less liquid a stock was in 2022, the more it rose. This is the clearest signature.
   A stock with almost no trading in 2022 is only in the file if something large happened
   to it later.

HOW IT AFFECTS MY STRATEGY

The daily liquidity filter stops the strategy buying a stock before it qualifies. It
cannot undo the fact that a stock which later qualifies is already known to survive.
Three estimates, from three angles, all on the final task2.py. Rows are means over 4
rebalance offsets, except the ghost row, which is 12 random seeds at the official offset
(official Sharpe 3.05).

   Test                                     Sharpe         CAGR            Max DD
   --------------------------------------   ------------   -------------   ---------
   As submitted, floor Rs 10 cr             2.97           46.6%           -10.9%
   Floor Rs 25 cr                           2.66           40.5%           -7.5%
   Floor Rs 50 cr                           1.92           27.6%           -13.4%
   Floor Rs 100 cr                          1.64           23.7%           -13.2%
   Every stock loses 29% a year             1.57           16.1%           -7.8%
   Up to 60 collapsing "ghost" stocks       2.99 to 3.05   47.6% to 48.4%  about -11%

   Higher floors remove the names most exposed to the bias. The 29% row removes most of
   the universe's tailwind. The ghost row measures the cost of holding failures.

The ghost test shows almost no cost. The clones fall 60% to 90% and vanish, but the
strategy rarely holds one when it falls and exits losers quickly. Direct losses from
missing failures are small here.

The larger effect is inflated winners. The top 10 trades make 57% of net profit, and names
like these are the ones the list flatters most. The liquidity gradient captures that.

My rough estimate: survivorship flatters this strategy by something like 19 points of CAGR
and 1.0 of Sharpe, the gap between the Rs 10 cr and Rs 50 cr floors. This is a guide, not a
bound. The gap includes any genuine small-cap effect, which overstates the bias, and it
ignores the bias still present above Rs 50 cr, which understates it. The hidden window has
the same bias, because its list is fixed at its end, but a live portfolio would not.


HOW I WOULD MEASURE IT PROPERLY

1. Build a point-in-time universe. Use NSE daily bhavcopies, which include later-delisted
   and suspended securities, apply the same liquidity rule each day, and re-run the
   strategy. The difference from this universe is the bias.

2. Failing that, compare with a point-in-time benchmark. Compare the equal-weight liquid
   universe with an index whose past membership is fixed, such as Nifty 500 Equal Weight,
   over the same window. The gap is the universe-level bias, which can be scaled by the
   strategy's exposure. The pack has no such index, so I have not done this.

3. Treat any delisted name as a loss. In the point-in-time run, mark the last price of a
   delisted stock at a heavy discount rather than holding it at the last close, since an
   investor often cannot exit at that price.
